⚠ Key Finding

December shows a significant drop in Email → MQL conversion (52.6% → 43.1%), a 9.5 percentage point decline. This is the most notable anomaly in the dataset and points to a data quality or pipeline issue rather than a demand-side shift. MQL → SQL also dropped from 50% to 44.1%.

Biggest Opportunity

MQL → SQL conversion is the highest-leverage lever. At ~48% conversion, nearly half of qualified prospects who show intent never become SQLs. Even a 10pp improvement here (+$X in pipeline) outpaces fixing Email → MQL given MQLs represent already-verified, high-intent users. The MQL → SQL gap likely reflects friction in the handoff to sales or self-serve application UX.

Email Volume vs. MQL Count — The Divergence

Root Cause Analysis

The volume went UP but MQL rate went DOWN — this is the key signal. December had 647 email submissions (+13.5% vs November's 570), yet MQLs dropped to 279 from 300. Two possible explanations:

Hypothesis A (Most Likely)

Holiday / seasonal traffic brought in lower-quality leads. December brings end-of-year campaigns, holiday promotions, and curiosity-driven visitors who don't represent qualified businesses. The extra 77 email submissions vs. November were disproportionately from unqualified companies (below the $75K bank balance threshold). This is a supply-mix shift, not a funnel breakdown.

Hypothesis B

MQL verification pipeline delays in December. If the MQL qualification check (bank balance verification) had processing delays or lower staffing over the holiday period, some leads that should be MQLs may have been marked as non-MQL or not yet processed by month-end close. This would also explain why SQL rate fell — fewer MQLs in the pipeline → fewer SQLs.

What's Specific

The Email → MQL drop (43.1% vs 52.6% baseline) is a 9.5pp decline. With 647 emails, if December had held the baseline 52.6% rate, we would have expected 340 MQLs instead of 279 — that's 61 missing MQLs. At the Nov MQL→SQL rate of 50%, those 61 MQLs would have yielded ~30 additional SQLs. December underdelivered by ~30 SQLs relative to the run-rate.

Recommended Action

Check the traffic source mix for December (paid vs. organic, campaign tags) to confirm whether a holiday campaign drove low-quality volume. If verified, implement traffic source–based lead scoring and suppress or segment low-intent December campaign traffic. Also audit the MQL verification SLA to detect any holiday processing delays.

Stage 1 · Visitor → Email

Progressive Disclosure Email Capture with Value-First Preview

Replace the current email wall with a "preview" experience: show 30 seconds of personalized value (estimated savings, peer benchmarks by company size/industry) before asking for an email. The ask becomes "unlock your full report" rather than a cold form. Add exit-intent trigger as a secondary capture point.

Stage 2 · Email → MQL

Real-Time Company Qualification Signal in the Email Form

Use the company domain from a work email to surface a real-time "looks like a match" or "let's check eligibility" message before submission. Pre-qualify via Clearbit/ZoomInfo enrichment at form submission — reducing unqualified submissions while increasing confidence for qualified users. Bonus: filter out personal emails (gmail, yahoo) at form level.

Stage 3 · MQL → SQL

Instant Scheduling + Guided Self-Serve Application Flow

For MQLs, replace the current handoff (likely an email follow-up sequence) with an immediate post-qualification page offering: (a) a Calendly-style instant demo booking with <24h slots, or (b) a guided 5-step self-serve application with progress save. The goal is to capture intent at peak enthusiasm immediately after qualification, not 48h later via cold email.

Deep Dive: Experiments 2 & 3

Projected Impact Comparison

Experiment 2 · Email → MQL

Reasonable conversion lift: +8–12pp (from ~48% to ~56–60%).

Why it works: By surfacing qualification signals inline, we shift from a "spray and pray" collection model to intention-verified submission. Users from qualifying companies get positive reinforcement; users with personal emails or sub-threshold companies self-select out. This improves MQL rate without reducing email volume from quality prospects.

Key risks: (1) Form friction could reduce total email submissions by 5–15%; (2) Clearbit/enrichment data has coverage gaps (~20–30% miss rate); (3) False negatives could reject valid prospects.

Experiment 3 · MQL → SQL

Reasonable conversion lift: +10–20pp (from ~48% to ~58–68%).

Why it works: The MQL → SQL gap is fundamentally a timing and friction problem. When prospects qualify, they're at peak intent. Every hour of delay cools that intent. Instant scheduling removes the "I'll do it later" escape hatch. A guided self-serve application removes the need for a human touchpoint entirely for lower-complexity prospects.

Key risks: (1) Sales team buy-in needed for scheduling tool; (2) Self-serve flow must be well-designed or it increases drop-off; (3) Some prospects may prefer async email outreach.

Decision & Data Gaps

Which experiment to run · What we're missing

Recommendation: Run Experiment 3 (MQL → SQL)

Given bandwidth constraints, Experiment 3 (Instant Scheduling + Guided Self-Serve for MQLs) is the clear choice.

The rationale: MQLs are already verified, high-intent, and expensive to acquire. Losing ~50% of them at the final handoff is the single largest dollar leak in the funnel. Fixing it requires no third-party data dependency, is lower-risk, and the change is reversible. Expected lift of 10–20pp on a baseline ~48% rate, applied to ~300 MQLs/month, yields 30–60 additional SQLs per month — the highest incremental SQL output of any experiment.

Experiment 2 is compelling but has a dependency on enrichment data quality and introduces form friction risk that could reduce top-of-funnel volume — a harder tradeoff to manage with limited bandwidth.

  • No third-party data dependency

What We're Missing

Website Visitor Counts Top-of-funnel metric (Visitor → Email rate) is entirely absent. We need session data from Google Analytics or Mixpanel to close this gap.
Traffic Source / UTM Tags Without knowing whether leads came from paid, organic, referral, or direct, we can't diagnose the December anomaly or optimize acquisition mix.
Time-to-SQL How long does it take an MQL to become an SQL? Knowing lag time would validate whether timing friction is the core problem and size the opportunity for instant scheduling.
MQL Drop Reason Why do ~50% of MQLs not convert to SQL? Is it sales capacity, prospect disengagement, price sensitivity, or competitive loss? A simple exit survey or CRM disposition tag would clarify enormously.
Industry-Level Conversion The dataset has Tech vs. Non-Tech, but no conversion breakdown by industry. If Tech companies convert at 2x the rate, the optimal strategy (traffic source, messaging) would differ significantly.
Revenue / ACV per SQL Without deal value data, we can't calculate the dollar impact of conversion improvements. A 10pp lift means very different things if average ACV is $10K vs. $100K.
Email Form Placement Data Which pages / forms are collecting the most (and highest-quality) emails? Without this, Experiment 1 (progressive disclosure) has no starting point for where to intervene first.
Longer Time Series Three months of data is too short to distinguish seasonality from trends. 12+ months would let us model expected December seasonality vs. a genuine conversion problem.
